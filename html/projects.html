● Machine Learning Optimization of Dynamical State Detector

Starting January 2027

Thakur et. al. (2024) introduced a machine-learning approach for detecting dynamical states using recurrence measures derived directly from nonlinear time series. Unlike conventional approaches that often require knowledge of the governing differential equations to calculate quantities such as Lyapunov exponents, their method uses recurrence-based features to train supervised classifiers.
My project, supervised by Dr. Chandrakala Meena, focuses on automating and optimising the preprocessing involved in extracting recurrence measures using machine learning, with the aim of streamlining the overall classification workflow.
It is a different trajectory from the other cosmology projects described below, and a welcome change of pace from my continuing cosmology projects!

●Classifying Sub-filaments using DisPerSE

Completion after my Endsems. Lo, the endless cycle of academic evaluvation!

Dhawalikar et. al. (2026) recently introduced a geometric classification scheme to classify sub-filmanets embedded within the cosmic filaments in the hierarchical cosmic web. A novel filament finder named Skeletor was used in this analysis. To better understand the nature of sub-filaments and the classification scheme used, I am attempting to use DisPerSE for the same, fully recognizing Skeletor as the optimal analysis software.

● Quenching in filament proximate mergers

Compare quenching proxies (e.g. D4000 index) of merging pairs as function of d_filament, controlling for stellar mass and cluster proximity. Tests whether the filament environment specifically drives quenching in merger pairs. This is a gap I found in Dulcien et al. (2026).
